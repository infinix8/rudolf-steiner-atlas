# Executive research summary

This document is a source-linked research atlas of Rudolf Steiner’s statements about the evolutionary origin, spiritual formation, present activity and future transformation of human organs. It consolidates material scattered across his cosmological writings, lectures on spiritual hierarchies, esoteric lessons, occult physiology, medical lectures and teachings about the Sefirot. It is an expanded structured synthesis, not a claim that Steiner published one unified organ-history table. This edition adds an eight-lecture GA 128 guide, new organ dossiers, a claim register, and explicit correction/tension notes.

Steiner’s characteristic idea is that the human physical body is older than today’s personal self-consciousness: its rudiments underwent Old Saturn, Old Sun, Old Moon and Earth development. The etheric body entered with Old Sun; the astral with Old Moon; and the individual “I” with Earth. He therefore treats organ structures as the cumulative result of successive cosmic transformations rather than as products of one single stage. [S01] [S02]

- Organ systems have an explicit chronology: sensory rudiments (Saturn); growth, nutrition, reproduction and glandular organization (Sun); nervous organization (Moon); and warm blood, renewed outward senses and individualized ego-organization (Earth). [S01] [S06]
- A separate sevenfold correspondence places the heart with Sun, brain with Moon, spleen with Saturn, liver with Jupiter, gallbladder with Mars, kidneys with Venus and lungs with Mercury. Steiner presents this as an analogical map of functional relationships, not an organ-by-organ date of origin. [S07] [S11]
- An unusually specific organ-and-beings association appears in GA 110: the early heart foundation is related to Leo and the activity of Thrones, Cherubim and Seraphim; the protective rib region to Cancer. This does not imply they were the only influences on the later heart. [S04]
- For ongoing activity, an esoteric lesson associates elemental helpers of Spirits of Wisdom with the heart and circulation, and helpers of Spirits of Movement and Form with the head and thinking. This source is a lesson record, not a laboratory description. [S12]
- In GA 128, an “organ” may name not just tissue but a supersensible force-complex that, in Steiner’s view, takes up material substance and participates in nutrition, regulation and awareness. [S10]
- The physical body’s “wisdom” is illustrated with the load-bearing architecture of the upper femur, and with the coordinated heart and brain; the “I” is depicted as younger and still learning to transform its inherited bodily organization. [S01] [S05]

Scope and limits. This research is restricted to Steiner’s own text or lecture/lesson notes housed by the Rudolf Steiner Archive. Later Anthroposophical authors are not used to supply missing links. Assertions about spiritual entities, reincarnation, occult organ-functions and prehistoric planetary conditions are reported as Steiner’s teachings—not as verified anatomy, physiology, evolutionary history or medical practice. Several medical assertions in the source texts conflict with established science and must not guide diagnosis or treatment.

# 1 | How to read the atlas

## 1.1 Four different kinds of organ statements

| Layer | The question it answers | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Origin / cosmic era | When did a precursor or functional principle arise? | The nervous system first receives rudiments on Old Moon. [S01] |
| Spiritual agency | Which beings does Steiner associate with formation or sustaining activity? | First hierarchy and the Leo region in the heart account. [S04] |
| Planetary analogy | Which organ mirrors a planetary function in the microcosm? | Spleen—Saturn; liver—Jupiter. [S07] |
| Present/future process | What does an organ do spiritually now, or become later? | Heart/circulation in the 1914 lesson; future voluntary heart. [S12] [S01] |

These layers must not be silently collapsed. “The heart is Sun” in the 1908 analogy does not mean that the adult anatomical heart existed on Old Sun, just as “the brain is Moon” is not a literal assertion that the contemporary brain was imported from the present Moon.

## 1.2 Evidence labels

- DIRECT: Steiner explicitly names an organ and an era, agency, planetary analogy or activity in a cited passage.
- SYNTHESIS: More than one primary passage supports a careful connection; the relationship is our organization of his claims, not his own printed table.
- OPEN: The consulted corpus has not yet supplied an explicit, securely verified connection; no invented mapping is inserted.
- TENSION: Two passages use different levels or orderings; the apparent difference is documented rather than erased.

## 1.3 Textual safeguards

The archive contains translations of different dates and styles, including lecture transcriptions and esoteric notes of uneven editorial completeness. Short quotations are kept rare, and all substantive claims carry source IDs. Translated words such as “spleen,” “glands,” “spirits,” “etheric” and “form” may vary; readers should compare the original German before drawing fine terminological conclusions. “Astral,” “etheric” and “I-organization” are Steiner’s technical terms.

Steiner calls the physical organization “most perfect” not because it is spiritually highest, but because, by his reckoning, it has undergone the longest series of transformations. The etheric body has three such stages behind it; the astral, two; the I, one. His point is developmental seniority, not moral superiority. [S01]

> “the physical body is, in its own kind, more perfect” — [S01]

## 2.1 Why the organs do not appear in a simple order on Earth

A major warning appears in GA 102 (24 March 1908). Steiner himself raises an objection: if the great eras introduced senses, then glands, then nerves, then blood, why do descriptions of early Earth sometimes begin with a warmth/blood organization and describe the other systems arising later? His response is that Earth recapitulates the earlier stages in a changed—partly reversed—order. The earlier organizations are continually metamorphosing, and the embryonic “blood” of the beginning is not yet literal ordinary blood. This is a key interpretive constraint when assembling timelines. [S06]

Thus, one should say “a sensory precursor was present in the Old Saturn condition” rather than “the modern eyeball existed on Saturn”; and “the blood principle is the distinctive Earth development” rather than imagine its mature present anatomical form appearing instantly. [S01] [S06]

# 3 | Spiritual agencies: who works on the human organization?

## 3.1 The four-stage formative account

| Stage / level | Steiner’s named agents | Kind of work he attributes | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Old Saturn | Spirits of Will / Thrones | Offering or outpouring warmth substance from which a primordial physical template is possible | [S03] [S02] |
| Old Sun | Spirits of Wisdom / Dominions | Offering the etheric life-principle; renewal of earlier physical rudiments | [S04] [S02] |
| Old Moon | Spirits of Movement (in wider hierarchy narrative) | Associated with further soul/astral participation and transformation; do not infer ownership of each nerve | [S02] |
| Earth | Spirits of Form and wider hierarchies | Participation in conditions for distinct form and ego development; no universal one-being-per-organ table | [S02] [S21] |

The above is an overview of cosmic agencies, not proof that each named hierarchy directly fashioned each named organ. In GA 110, Steiner explicitly says the first heart foundation involved the Thrones, Cherubim and Seraphim acting from the Leo region. This provides a stronger organ-specific example than the broad planetary-era scheme. [S04]

## 3.2 A verified specific case: the heart and the zodiac

In the April 17, 1909 lecture, Steiner interprets the human heart’s earliest seed as formed in connection with the Leo region of the zodiac and the activity of the first hierarchy (Thrones, Cherubim, Seraphim). He also connects the protective bony breast/chest region with Cancer, the crab and its shell. His claim concerns a spiritual archetypal genesis; he does not supply a modern embryological or physical causal chain. [S04]

This passage is easy to overextend. It supports an explicit Leo–heart and Cancer–rib-region correspondence, but not a complete twelve-sign-to-twelve-organ chart that can safely be attributed to the same lecture without further verification. [S04]

## 3.3 Not only past builders: beings said to work now

In the esoteric lesson dated April 25, 1914, Steiner links elemental helpers sent by the Spirits of Movement and Spirits of Form to the head and thinking; other elemental helpers, associated with the Spirits of Wisdom, to the heart and blood circulation; and still others to warmth and word perception. The lesson begins by reflecting on sleep, during which the astral body and I are said to withdraw while the physical/etheric organizations remain. This is an explicit present-activity account, not just a creation story. [S12]

In GA 235 (March 2, 1924), he adds a different but related threefold organization: activities of the third hierarchy in sense/head organization, the second hierarchy in the rhythmic/heart region, and the first hierarchy in the metabolic-limb organization. Those functional associations coexist with rather than replace the earlier Leo–heart origin statement. [S21]

# 5 | Individual dossiers

## 5.1 Heart — a long history with different kinds of agency

ORIGIN: In GA 110, the earliest cardiac seed is linked to the zodiacal Leo region and to Thrones, Cherubim and Seraphim. The related “Cancer” passage refers to the protective chest/rib organization. This is the clearest direct association between a named hierarchy, a specific organ and an archetypal region found in this research pass. [S04]

COSMIC ANALOGY: In GA 107 and the final GA 128 lecture the heart functions as the Sun of an “inner planetary system,” standing in reciprocal relation to the spleen–Saturn, liver–Jupiter and gallbladder–Mars, and also to lungs–Mercury and kidneys–Venus. The analogy expresses coordination and mutual influence among organ-functions, not a literal little solar system inside the chest. [S07] [S11]

CURRENT ACTIVITY: The April 1914 lesson describes elemental emissaries of Spirits of Wisdom as at work in the heart and circulation. The statement is about sustaining spiritual activity and is distinguishable from the earlier account of heart-origin through the first hierarchy. [S12]

FUTURE: GA 11 and GA 56 claim that the heart will become an increasingly consciously directed organ, metaphorically or esoterically described as a future voluntary muscle. This contradicts ordinary physiology if treated as a prediction of imminent bodily control. It belongs to Steiner’s far-future developmental scheme. [S01] [S20]

OPEN: These citations do not give a single exhaustive stage-by-stage chronology of the anatomical heart. Nor is the specific heart-with-Leo example proof that a planetary ruler, zodiacal source and spiritual hierarchy are interchangeable labels.

## 5.2 Spleen — why Steiner treats it as an unusually spiritual organ

PLANETARY LINK: The spleen is Saturn in Steiner’s internal planetary system. In GA 128 he presents it as the first major organ-system to confront the blood/nutritional process in his schematic picture; alongside liver and gallbladder it helps transform material from the outer world for internal use. [S07] [S08] [S11]

FORCE VERSUS TISSUE: Steiner makes the spleen his prime example of a supersensible organ-function not identical with anatomical material. He argues that a physical spleen can sometimes be surgically removed without wholly eliminating the corresponding functional field. This is a metaphysical inference he uses to explain what he calls the physical expression of an organ. [S09] [S10]

CAUTION: Splenectomy does not make the physical spleen biologically unimportant; contemporary medicine recognizes serious infection risks and other effects. This dossier reports Steiner’s distinction, not a medical endorsement of his explanation.

OPEN: “Spleen = Saturn” is attested. “The spleen was separately built by the Thrones during Old Saturn” is not established by the cited passages and should not be asserted without a direct text.

## 5.3 Liver — Jupiter and inward transformation

PLANETARY AND FUNCTIONAL LINK: The liver is the Jupiter member of the internal cosmic system. In GA 128 Steiner places it between the nutritional stream and the broader blood/organ economy, in a relational triad with spleen and gallbladder. He explains the significance chiefly through processes, not isolated anatomical anatomy. [S07] [S08] [S09] [S11]

REINCARNATION: In GA 205 he assigns to the liver organization forces connected with thought and disposition that, in his account, can be carried toward the next earthly life through a wider transformation involving the metabolic-limb organism. This belongs to his karma doctrine and is not an established mechanism of memory or heredity. [S13]

OPEN: No verified claim in the selected sources tells us that the adult liver arose as an individual organ on Old Jupiter. “Future Jupiter” in Steiner’s seven-stage chronology and the present planetary analogy “liver–Jupiter” are different reference systems.

## 5.4 Gallbladder and bile — Mars within a nutritional triad

Steiner’s correspondence is gallbladder (or gall/bile function) with Mars. His main account is relational: the spleen–Saturn, liver–Jupiter and gallbladder–Mars transform and individualize what enters through nutrition before the inner human economy engages the wider outside world. He stresses the organs’ relationships to blood and the heart rather than proposing a self-contained “Mars organ” independent of the whole. [S07] [S08] [S09] [S11]

Where he turns to occult medicines and metals, Mars is also related to iron. Those assertions are historical esoteric therapeutics, not validated prescribing instructions. The current documentary record lacks a uniquely assigned spiritual hierarchy or a specific first planetary era for the gallbladder. [S11]

## 5.5 Lungs — Mercury and contact with the outside world

In the 1911 inner-system description, the lungs stand on the side of the heart that opens directly toward the outer environment: breathing brings an external stream into relation with blood, while the nutritional organs transform a different stream from within. This is why Steiner pairs lungs–Mercury with kidneys–Venus as balancing counterparts to the spleen/liver/gall group. [S09] [S11]

In GA 205 the lung organization has a second, karmic significance: its inner forces allegedly preserve aspects of the soul’s relation to outer objects and contribute, after death, to the outward form of a head in a subsequent incarnation. That claim is distinct from the Mercury analogy and should be indexed under “between lives,” not “organ origin.” [S13]

OPEN: His lectures do not give a direct “Mercury created the lungs on Old Sun” formula in the sources reviewed.

## 5.6 Kidneys — Venus and the mediation of inner and outer streams

Steiner assigns kidneys to Venus in the planetary mapping. More importantly, GA 128 describes a mediating or equilibrating kidney-system between nutrients transformed inwardly and air-derived influences encountered through the lungs and blood. It represents a relation within the whole organism, not a symbolic label floating free from bodily function. [S09] [S11]

GA 205 adds a further claim: influences linked to the kidney organization relate to enduring habits, temperament and forces carried toward future incarnation. In the same lecture he contrasts lungs/earth, liver/water, kidneys/air and heart/warmth. These element associations should not be confused with the older Saturn–Sun–Moon–Earth chronology. [S13]

## 5.7 Brain, nerves and the organization of thought

DEVELOPMENTAL PERIOD: The first rudiments of a nervous system are located by Steiner on Old Moon, when the astral body enters. GA 11 emphasizes that this is not the present-day nervous system unchanged. With Earth, consciousness becomes outwardly object-directed; Steiner associates the Earth-stage I with the transformation of the entire inherited organization. [S01]

ANALOGY: His 1908 organ/planet list names the brain as Moon, but this is an etheric correspondence and must not be turned into the claim that a modern brain emerged literally on today’s Moon. [S07]

AGENTS: A 1906 cosmology lecture associates a development of brain-organization with “Spirits of Fire” or “Spirits of Air” in a story of older human streams. The 1914 lesson associates helpers sent by Spirits of Form and Movement with the head and thinking. These are different narratives and should remain separately linked. [S25] [S12]

FORMATION: The January 1924 medical lecture portrays the head as especially open to the surrounding cosmos, contrasting it with long bones and the Earth. The March 1915 lecture imagines early lunar mobile organizations eventually becoming fixed in a physical brain, freeing spiritual-soul capacities for thought. These claims are esoteric, not descriptions of known neurodevelopment. [S14] [S22]

## 5.8 Eye and sight — two different ideas about origin

GA 11 locates very early physical precursors of sense organs, including the eye, within Old Saturn. GA 96 offers a more differentiated account of distinct sensory faculties, putting the development of sight on Old Sun. These are not automatically contradictory: a physical precursor and a specialized perceptual faculty are different levels in Steiner’s explanation. [S01] [S05]

In GA 119 and GA 100, Steiner develops the idea that light is an eye-forming influence, concealing a higher power that helps call the organ into being. He takes inspiration from Goethe’s formula that the eye is formed for light by light. These passages do not, by themselves, identify a particular angelic choir as the sole builder of the eye. [S17] [S18]

For atlas purposes, label the entries: “sensory material prototype: Saturn,” “specialized seeing faculty: Sun” and “current formative environment: light.” Without these fields, a one-word entry “Eye—Saturn” or “Eye—Sun” loses a critical distinction.

## 5.9 Ear, hearing and balance — different spiritual “ages” of the senses

Steiner’s October 1906 lecture explicitly calls hearing the oldest of the listed senses and describes the primordial human organization as an all-over resonating “ear”; warmth perception is linked to Saturn, seeing to Sun, tasting to Moon and smelling to Earth. Here “ear” initially means a diffuse perceptive/resonant capacity, not the later cochlea or eardrum. [S05]

The November 1906 lecture connects the ear with a still older spatial-orientation sense, embodied in the semicircular canals, and distinguishes this from hearing tones. This offers a nuanced hierarchy of perceptual histories, not a conventional anatomy chronology. [S16]

IMPORTANCE: In GA 96 he describes the senses as having different degrees of “perfection” because they have been transformed different numbers of times. These are his spiritual-development rankings and should not be misread as comparative scientific ratings of sensory systems. [S05]

## 5.10 Femur, skull and ribs — where cosmic and earthly form meet

In GA 11 Steiner calls attention to the inner strut-like architecture of the upper femur, emphasizing load-bearing strength with economical use of material. For him this is a perceptible example of the long inherited “wisdom” of bodily form. Actual bone architecture is studied through mechanics, development and remodeling, and cannot demonstrate a spiritual hierarchy. [S01]

GA 316 presents a polarity: skull/brain as relatively cosmic in formation, long limbs and their tubular bones as particularly affected by Earth, and ribs as intermediate. It is a system-level interpretation of shape and forces, not an organ-specific geological history. [S14]

In GA 353 he uses the tubular femur to illustrate Malkuth—the tenth Sefirah as Earth’s distinctive action on the human organism. He contrasts this with Yesod, the foundation associated with being able to stand. Again, this is a spiritual reading of visible anatomy, not a verified account of how cavities in long bones develop. [S15]

## 5.11 Larynx and reproductive organization — a proposed future metamorphosis

GA 99 explicitly describes a laryngeal precursor on Old Saturn; its later vocal capacities emerge through subsequent stages. This corrects the older OPEN entry. [S35]

GA 11 places the earliest general growth/nutrition/reproduction organization under Old Sun conditions, when the etheric body is added to an inherited physical template. It does not give a fully formed reproductive organ at that time. [S01]

In later lectures, especially GA 56, Steiner imagines an extremely distant future in which the larynx acquires a creative function and the heart comes under greater volitional direction. He links changes in reproduction and speech to transformations of the human being. These are speculative spiritual-evolution claims, not biomedical predictions about an individual living today. [S20]

OPEN: A detailed organ-by-organ account of reproductive tissues and their angelic builders remains unverified in this study. A comprehensive atlas should search targeted lectures and avoid extrapolating from the broad Old Sun reproductive-system claim.

## 5.12 Glandular and other intermediary organs

The glandular system belongs to the Old Sun layer of the cosmic account, where Steiner relates it to etheric life and the transformation of nutrition/growth. GA 96 also mentions a sympathetic nervous system developing on Old Sun even while GA 11 places the first nervous-system rudiments more generally on Old Moon. This indicates that he sometimes speaks of specific subsystems and sometimes of the dominant whole-system development; the atlas records rather than erases the difference. [S01] [S05]

In GA 105 he describes ancient influences of Sun and Moon on head organs including a gland he calls the mucous gland (historically associated with the pituitary in translations). These ideas are especially vulnerable to terminological and anatomical confusion. Until the German and other translations are compared, that statement is best kept as a research note rather than turned into a fixed modern endocrine-organ assignment. [S19]

# 6 | How spiritual activity is said to continue in the present

## 6.1 An organ is more than its visible material

Steiner’s most useful methodological statement is in GA 128, lecture V. In his formulation, the visible spleen, kidney or liver is material taken up into a pre-existing supersensible force organization; the form and ongoing activity are not reducible to the organ’s material components. This is the overarching rationale for interpreting organs as records of prior cosmic activity that can also be worked upon now. [S10]

> “first planned as super-sensible organs” — [S10]

## 6.2 Activity during sleep

The April 1914 lesson begins with his account of the I and astral body outside the physical/etheric bodies in sleep. It then describes active elemental helpers as linked to the head, heart and senses. We should not infer from this single lesson that every organ has a specified roster of nocturnal workers; it gives concrete examples of a wider doctrine. [S12]

## 6.3 Activity between death and another birth

In GA 205, the physical tissue is said to be left behind at death while formative forces of the organism participate in the passage to a further life. Steiner gives particular attention to lungs, liver, kidneys and heart, relating different kinds of lived experience to the form of a later head and temperament. This is not the same as saying a spirit rebuilds the same physical heart each night; it is a different temporal and karmic mechanism. [S13]

## 6.4 Activity in the “I” during life

GA 11 distinguishes the already sophisticated inherited bodily organization from the younger personal I, which can transform astral tendencies into spirit-self, etheric habits into life-spirit and ultimately the physical body into spirit-man. On Steiner’s terms, individual freedom gradually participates in the wisdom formerly supplied through a much longer, largely unconscious evolution. [S01]

## 7.2 The Sefirot are a connected, but distinct, explanatory layer

In GA 353 Steiner explains ten Sefirot as an “alphabet” of forces approaching the human being: three predominantly linked to head, three to chest/rhythm, three to movement/limbs and Malkuth to the Earth’s own contribution. He specifically refers to the thigh-bone tube under Malkuth. [S15]

An editorial note in GA 89 records a 1908 statement connecting ten Sefirot with four physical-body stages + three etheric-body stages + two astral-body stages + one I stage. This is an explicit numerical bridge to the planetary chronology, but it is not a complete Sefirah-by-organ-by-era correspondence. [S23]

The most rigorous atlas should therefore offer a graph with labeled relationships (“arises-in,” “corresponds-to,” “formed-by,” “influenced-now-by,” “future-metamorphosis,” “illustrates-Sefirah”) rather than one oversized table implying these relations are equivalent.

# 8 | Deep reading: all eight lectures of GA 128 (Prague, March 1911)

This expanded guide follows the complete eight-lecture argument of An Occult Physiology. It is especially useful because an isolated “liver = Jupiter” statement can be misunderstood unless we first follow how Steiner develops the relation between the senses, nerves, blood, internal organs, the I and bodily form. Each précis below is editorial synthesis, and all passages should be checked in the linked primary lecture. [S26] [S08] [S27] [S09] [S10] [S28] [S24] [S11]

## 8.1 Lecture I, 20 March: two polar organizations, skull and spinal cord

Steiner begins not with planets but with an anatomical contrast. Brain and spinal cord are enclosed inside protective bone, whereas the limbs, viscera and remainder of the trunk connect with them through other pathways. He resists inferring human organ functions from apparently similar organs in animals. He then distinguishes the brain as the instrument for delayed, reflective action from the spinal cord as an instrument of reflexlike response. His speculation that the spinal cord represents a new or incomplete attempt to form a brain must not be mistaken for mainstream neuroanatomy. The methodological point is that a human organ is interpreted within the entire organization and its spiritual history rather than from resemblance alone. [S26]

## 8.2 Lecture II, 21 March: the internal world condensed into organs

Steiner then contrasts the external world impressed upon us through eye, ear, brain and nervous activity with the “inner world” represented by spleen, liver and gallbladder. He describes the blood as meeting outside influences through the upper organism and a different kind of inward influence from the visceral organs. Crucially, he initially presents the planetary names as provisional designations: spleen–Saturn, liver–Jupiter, gallbladder–Mars. This passage is important evidence against reading these labels as literal dates of origin or as the proper names of exclusive builders. [S08]

## 8.3 Lecture III, 22 March: the sympathetic nerves and the two sides of blood

Here the conceptual mechanism becomes clearer. The sensory/nervous organization relates blood to impressions from outside, while the autonomic or sympathetic nervous organization mediates between enclosed internal organs and blood from within. He depicts the blood as a tablet receiving inscriptions from two directions. The liver, spleen and gallbladder form an inward counterpart to the eye and ear rather than ordinary organs of outward perception. He explicitly describes such organs as a contraction of the macrocosm into a human microcosm. This is a relational model; there is no claim that the spleen directly senses Saturn as the eye senses light. [S27]

## 8.4 Lecture IV, 23 March: heart, lungs and kidneys complete the inner world-system

The internal picture expands from the initial spleen–liver–gallbladder triad. Heart and circulating blood become the Sun of the inner system; lungs are named Mercury and kidneys Venus. The lungs connect blood to the surrounding air, while the kidneys are discussed in connection with bodily elimination and balance. The movement between nutrition, transformation, blood and breathing is more important to his argument than the mere sevenfold list. As with the planetary names in Lecture II, the point is correspondence and relationship, not a separate planetary “birth certificate” for each organ. [S09]

## 8.5 Lecture V, 24 March: visible organs versus supersensible force systems

This lecture supplies the interpretive key for the entire organ atlas. Steiner proposes that an organ is initially a formative force-organization that subsequently becomes invested with physical material. He treats the spleen as particularly dependent on what he calls a supersensible organ, whereas the physically functioning liver and kidneys are, in his account, more materially implicated. His remarks about removal of a spleen are evidence for his metaphysical distinction, not a medical assurance that spleen removal is harmless. Anatomical tissues and occult “force organs” must be separate data entities in the atlas. [S10]

## 8.6 Lecture VI, 26 March: skin, blood, bone and the human I

Steiner describes the skin as the body’s outer boundary and a kind of recapitulation of the whole organism. He finds there minute blood vessels (his instrument of the I), nerves (consciousness/astral activity), glands (etheric life and secretion), and material exchange (physical organization). He then contrasts the blood as a relatively new, mobile instrument of the I with the bony system as the extremely ancient, rigid culmination of prior formative work. He claims the physical framework had to be predisposed before glands, nerves and blood could be placed in an organism. This is among his clearest arguments that mature bodily organization records a much longer evolutionary history than the current conscious I. [S28]

The same lecture associates the shape of the individual head with formative forces carried from previous earthly incarnations. This is a particularly strong example of Steiner proposing concrete karmic consequences for anatomy. It is an esoteric assertion, not physical evidence for reincarnation; skull shape is not an accepted means of establishing past lives. [S28]

## 8.7 Lecture VII, 27 March: excessive activities and reciprocal compensation

Steiner moves from the cooperation of organs toward his occult understanding of illness and attempted balancing. He speaks of liver, spleen and gallbladder as one set of transformative processes, and lungs and kidneys as organs that prevent excessive inward activity through the movement of material outward. No single organ can be understood without the compensating processes of the others. His therapeutic language, including proposed uses of salts and metals, is historically significant but not valid clinical guidance. The atlas records its conceptual architecture without reproducing remedies or doses. [S24]

## 8.8 Lecture VIII, 28 March: why the heart occupies the center

The closing lecture draws the model together. In Steiner’s account, liver, gallbladder and spleen are predominantly associated with an ascending transformation of nutritive processes, while lungs and kidneys have a descending or expelling relationship. He warns against rigid classifications, noting that the lungs also admit an incoming influence. The heart, as internal Sun, stands in a balancing relation to both clusters. Only at this point does he develop the planetary analogies and corresponding traditional metal analogies more fully. He also says that upper brain–spinal functions developed in parallel with lower organ systems; he is not presenting an evolutionary sequence in which the lower organs were built and only afterward a head appeared. [S11]

# 9 | Expanded organ dossiers and new primary evidence

## 9.1 Larynx: the overlooked Old Saturn precursor

CORRECTION TO EDITION 1.0. GA 99, lecture VIII (1 June 1907), explicitly places a rudiment of the larynx on Old Saturn alongside the early sense organs. Steiner says it could not yet serve speech; he speaks of inarticulate vocalization during the Old Moon condition and of the later emergence of its present capacity. The important distinction is larynx as structural precursor, vocalization as intermediate capacity, and articulate human speech as later development. This makes the earlier entry “no specific initial era securely assigned” too cautious; the matrix should now read “laryngeal rudiment on Saturn; later vocal function” with a DIRECT label. [S35]

FUTURE: GA 56 presents a very distant spiritual-development scenario in which the larynx is tied to creative speech and transformations of reproduction, and the heart to conscious, voluntary function. This is not an empirical prediction about a short-term mutation of the human voice or heart muscle. [S20]

## 9.2 Skin: a microcosm of all four human members

The skin merits an independent organ dossier because GA 128, lecture VI, explicitly treats it as the outward limit where the whole human organization is expressed. He locates the I’s blood system, the astral nervous system, the etheric glandular processes and the physical transportation/deposition of substances within one organ field. This is an explicit present-organization mapping, not an assignment of “skin born on Saturn.” For a knowledge graph, link skin to “expresses: fourfold human organization” and to each system separately, with GA 128 VI as the evidence. [S28]

## 9.3 Bone and blood as opposite evolutionary ages

Steiner calls bone the oldest force-system, the framework toward which previous formation has moved; he calls blood a relative newcomer and instrument of the Earth-stage I. His point is not that contemporary hard bone literally existed before glands but that an earlier supportive principle was already present, subsequently transformed through later eras. Blood responds, in his account, to the movements of ego-life, whereas bone bears a deeply consolidated structure that ordinary will scarcely alters. He interprets the coexistence of the two systems as a visible record of an ancient–young polarity. [S28]

The femur example in GA 11, the skull/long-bone polarity in GA 316 and the tubular femur/Malkuth connection in GA 353 should each remain separately attached: engineering-like architecture, cosmic versus terrestrial formative forces, and Sefirah interpretation are three different claims. [S01] [S14] [S15]

## 9.4 Liver: Saturn influence versus Jupiter correspondence

IMPORTANT SOURCE TENSION. GA 128 calls the liver an inner Jupiter within a seven-organ relational model. In GA 102, lecture I (6 January 1908), Steiner explicitly says Saturn beings find expression in the liver and Saturn forces work strongly on it. He contrasts inwardly enclosing liver forces with breathing and the lungs as a more outward-opening or emancipating tendency, and interprets the Prometheus myth through this relationship. These are not interchangeable assertions. One is a macrocosmic organ analogy (Jupiter); the other is a claim of a Saturnian influence or agency upon the liver. The responsible conclusion is that “Jupiter organ” does not mean “organ solely governed or built by Jupiter”; further passages could reveal additional influences. [S08] [S36]

In GA 218, lecture of 22 October 1922, Steiner additionally assigns the liver a special relation to the human I. GA 205 links inward liver forces to what he says becomes the disposition of the next incarnation’s brain. These statements belong under present constitution and between-lives metamorphosis, respectively—not under the Old Jupiter planetary era. [S33] [S13]

## 9.5 Spleen: Saturn, regulation, etheric substitution and spirit-self

GA 128 contrasts the spleen’s supersensible force-character with the greater physical dependence of liver and kidneys. Later, in GA 312, lecture XV (4 April 1920), Steiner calls it a largely subconscious “sense” for the rhythm of nutrition and says it reconciles crude metabolic processes with more inward, mental or spiritual activity. This extends its atlas profile from a Saturn correspondence to the claimed regulation of meal rhythms. [S10] [S30]

In GA 218, he goes further and calls the spleen an organ associated with spirit-self, beyond the ordinary I. This is neither an alternative date of cosmic emergence nor a straightforward statement that the physical spleen is the anatomical location of a fully developed spirit-self. It is a claimed organ–higher-member correspondence; the exact level of literalness must be retained as an interpretive question. [S33]

## 9.6 Kidney: astral body, temperament and the air element

GA 218 directly associates kidneys with the astral body, contrasting the liver and its relation to the I. GA 205 adds that the kidneys preserve forces associated with lasting emotional habits and temperament, which Steiner says contribute to a future incarnation’s head organization. He also associates the kidney system with air in a four-element mapping where lungs are earth, liver water and heart warmth. The Venus correspondence remains a separate and earlier symbolic layer. [S33] [S13] [S11]

## 9.7 Lung: Mercury, breath, external impressions and future head form

GA 102, lecture I, calls the lungs a counter-organ to the liver because breathing repeatedly opens the human being to the outside world; it reads the myth of Prometheus in this light. GA 128 uses Mercury for the lungs’ place in the inner planetary system. GA 205 goes still further, claiming that forces connected with the representation of external objects are stored in lung organization and later help form the outward shape of the head in the next incarnation. These are three different explanatory scales: a polar functional contrast, a planetary analogy, and a theory of post-mortem metamorphosis. [S36] [S11] [S13]

## 9.8 Heart: Leo origin, Sun function, inner perception, karma and future freedom

The heart is the richest single dossier because Steiner repeatedly returns to it from distinct perspectives. GA 110: first foundation connected with Leo and the first hierarchy. GA 128: Sun of the internal organ system, related reciprocally to nutrition, breathing and elimination. GA 312, lecture II (22 March 1920): an organ of inward perception, expressing the equilibrium of nervous-sensory/breathing processes and metabolism. GA 205: warmth organization and a bearer of what he calls karmic forces into another life. GA 56 and GA 68b: future spiritual/volitional heart activity. None of these different claims gives a single complete uninterrupted story of heart development; all should be indexed together but not blended into a fabricated sequence. [S04] [S11] [S29] [S13] [S20] [S41]

MEDICAL CORRECTION AND CAUTION: Steiner explicitly denied the heart’s pumping function in his GA 205 exposition. That denial conflicts with established cardiovascular physiology. The organ atlas includes his statement as a historical record of his doctrine but does not validate it. The heart pumps blood; readers should not infer that a spiritual account supersedes its known physical function. [S13]

## 9.9 Brain and spinal cord: instruments, metamorphosis and individual history

In GA 128, lecture I, the brain is the instrument of more elaborate, delayed action following sense impressions, while the spinal cord mediates certain rapid/reflexive responses. In GA 128 VI he also presents the distinctive form of a skull as a karmic imprint of previous incarnation. The combined claims are not modern neurological science; they show how Steiner links an organ’s physical construction, its conscious function and its proposed inter-incarnation history. [S26] [S28]

In GA 205 he reverses the viewpoint and argues that the metabolic–limb forces of one life shape the head of the next. GA 208 explains the process through what he calls experiences in the spiritual world between death and rebirth. GA 141 likewise pictures the human being learning or contemplating bodily secrets during that interval. These texts are useful for an atlas because they describe WHY the mature body would embody wisdom for Steiner: its formative conditions are prepared beyond a single earthly lifetime. [S13] [S32] [S34]

## 9.10 Eyes, ears, equilibrium and twelvefold senses

GA 99 calls the Saturn human being a bearer of very early sense-organ rudiments and expressly includes eye, ear and larynx in that extended story. GA 96 distinguishes the “ages” of specialized sense faculties. GA 169, lecture of 20 June 1916, gives a twelve-sense scheme: touch, life, movement, balance, smell, taste, sight, warmth, hearing, speech, thinking and perception of another I. In that later teaching, the human sensory organization is connected analogically with the twelvefold zodiac. This is a different numerological/functional scheme from the seven inner organs and their planets. [S35] [S05] [S39]

GA 93a, 4 October 1905, proposes possible future sensory organs connected with the pituitary (hypophysis) and pineal (epiphysis). Record these as speculative future-sense claims with historically shifting terminology, not established neuroscience or endocrine predictions. The corpus contains different classifications of senses at different dates; avoid merging them into a single frozen doctrine without comparison. [S40]

## 9.11 Sympathetic nervous system, glands and hidden inward experience

The sympathetic nervous system emerges as a separate entry in GA 128, lecture III, where Steiner treats it as a mediator between the enclosed visceral world and blood, contrasted with the brain–spinal system related to external sense impressions. The glandular apparatus is another key system: GA 102 places its broad origins on Old Sun, while GA 128 VI relates glands and secretion to etheric life within the skin. A complete organ index must therefore include networks and regulatory processes, not just discrete named viscera. [S27] [S06] [S28]

## 9.12 Digestive tract, saliva, lymph and nutrient transformation

GA 128 builds the internal planetary picture around nutrition and blood; it is not simply a theory of organ shapes. In GA 218 (22 October 1922), Steiner traces nutrition from mouth and glandular secretions through digestive change, and describes the need for incoming plant/animal material to lose its previous organization before it can be taken up into human life. Whether or not one accepts his metaphysical account, this reveals why he places digestive glands, intestinal processes and blood alongside the large named organs. The book of organs should index metabolic transformations as processes with multiple organ participants. [S33] [S08] [S09]

## 9.13 Future development of organ functions

In his future-oriented passages, Steiner projects that the present I will more consciously work through the astral organization (spirit-self), etheric organization (life-spirit) and eventually physical organization (spirit-man). The envisioned future heart and larynx are special cases within that broader scheme. These are cosmic future claims, not instructions for trying to alter the heartbeat, larynx or other organs through willpower today. [S01] [S20] [S41]

# 13 | Interpretive cautions and corrected oversimplifications

- NEW CORRECTION: The 1907 GA 99 lecture does name a laryngeal precursor on Old Saturn. Edition 1.0 left this open; edition 2.0 should include it explicitly. [S35]
- NEW SOURCE TENSION: “Liver–Jupiter” (GA 128) and “Saturn beings active in liver” (GA 102) are simultaneously attested. A planet-organ analogy is not an exclusive spirit-to-organ assignment. [S08] [S36]
- NEW CROSS-CORRESPONDENCE: GA 218 associates the kidneys with the astral body, the liver with the I, and the spleen with spirit-self. This does not replace the historical planetary correspondences. [S33]
- IMPORTANT: Steiner’s 1911 model is concerned with multiple metabolic and nervous exchanges, not merely seven organ labels. Its “inner world” and “outer world” polarity explains why these organs matter together. [S27] [S11]
- IMPORTANT: The GA 205 theories about lungs, liver, kidneys and heart and future incarnation are distinct from developmental claims about Old Saturn–Old Moon. [S13]
- IMPORTANT: The 1916 twelve-sense scheme and the 1905 future pineal/pituitary senses have separate dates, contexts and vocabularies. One should not force them into an unchanging seven-senses chronology. [S39] [S40]
- HEALTH WARNING: Claims that the heart does not pump, that the organs generate psychiatric conditions, or that metals treat occult organ imbalances are historically reported as Steiner’s statements; they are contradicted or unsupported by modern medical knowledge and must not guide personal healthcare. [S13] [S24]
- RESEARCH LIMIT: This is a deeper primary-source pass through central organ lectures, not an exhaustive full-text concordance of every mention in Steiner’s thousands of lectures. Source-level links, traceability and explicitly open questions take priority over claims of total coverage.

# 14 | Research issues, tensions and unverified claims

- Apparent reversal: GA 11 describes the planetary sequence senses–glands–nerves–blood; GA 102 explicitly argues that the Earth recapitulation does not repeat this linearly. The difference is addressed by Steiner himself. [S01] [S06]
- Precursor vs faculty: sensory material rudiments in Saturn (GA 11) and specialized sight during Sun (GA 96) are statements at different developmental levels. [S01] [S05]
- Heart’s multiple agents: first-hierarchy/Leo origin (GA 110), Wisdom-associated current circulation (GA 266) and the second hierarchy in rhythmic organization (GA 235) describe different times or functional perspectives; no exclusivity is asserted. [S04] [S12] [S21]
- “Brain—Moon” in GA 107 is a correspondence; nervous-system emergence during Old Moon in GA 11 is a separate chronology statement. [S07] [S01]
- Earlier conversation shorthand such as “the organ was created by a particular planet” is too broad. Only direct wording and context justify organ-specific origin or agency.
- Limited coverage: definitive independent era dates for individual adult spleen, liver, gallbladder, lungs and kidneys have not been established from reviewed passages; they should remain OPEN rather than be guessed.
- Steiner’s occult therapeutics, proposed causes of hallucinations, and claims about internal organs are not validated medical science. They should be studied historically, never used to self-diagnose, change medication, or replace professional care. [S11] [S13] [S24]

## Extraction unit: one atomic attributable claim

Each record stores: stable passage ID; GA number, title, exact lecture date and city; authored/lecture/notes distinction; language/translation; canonical Archive URL; paragraph/heading/nearby-text cue; organ names (normalized and original wording); cited agent or hierarchy; planetary or zodiac reference; developmental scale (cosmic era / embryology / childhood / adult / nightly sleep / between lives / future); relation type; concise editorial paraphrase; short quotation only if necessary; confidence/evidence status; original German check; comparison group; editorial caution; and review history.

**Relation types must not collapse into one field**: `begins_development_on`, `transforms_during`, `fashioned_by`, `sustained_by`, `planetary_correspondence`, `member_of_human_constitution`, `organ_senses`, `metamorphoses_into`, `reflects_activity_of`, `participates_in_sleep`, `reincarnational_function`, `medical_therapeutic_claim`, `comparison_not_equivalence`, and `open_question`.

## Evidence labels and review gates

1. `lead` — a search hit, topic index or book list; **not** passage evidence.
2. `located` — date, page and thematic passage found; needs checking in context.
3. `selected_passage_checked` — the actual passage read in context and narrowly summarized; **not** entire-lecture coverage.
4. `lecture_audited` — a reader has inspected the whole lecture, including passing mentions and negatives, and recorded coverage.
5. `translation_checked` — original German and at least one English translation compared where the distinction matters.
6. `reviewed` — second pass checked the original attribution, typed edges and contrast notes.
7. `disputed` — contradictory translation, attribution or interpretive problem; preserve rather than erase.

Citation must point directly to the passage-bearing original lecture URL. Use the Archive's numbered paragraphs if available; otherwise a concise distinctive section cue and a verified nearby phrase. Store no extended source text, only original summaries and brief licensed quotations.

## Six practical workstreams

**A. Corpus inventory and completeness ledger.** Create a GA-by-GA table with lecture date, language and translation variants; avoid double counting alternate Archive views as separate lectures. For every entry store `unread`, `skimmed`, `passage_indexed`, `whole_lecture_audited`, `German_collated`, `peer_reviewed`. Log archive availability and source-quality warnings.

**B. Discovery and controlled vocabulary.** Search organ names (including anatomical variants), body-process words (respiration, liver process, spleen process, assimilation, circulation), German cognates and Steiner's specialized phrases (`Wärmeorganismus`, `Ich-Organisation`, `ätherisch`, `astralisch`, `Leber`, `Niere`, `Milz`, `Kehlkopf`, `Knochen`). Search *also* agency names and technical relations, because many important passages speak of organs without a single standard organ keyword.

**C. Passage extraction and interpretation.** Read the surrounding paragraphs, extract narrowly and attribute exactly. Capture whether Steiner is literally describing organs, making a Goethean analogy, proposing medical treatment, presenting a cosmic-history claim, or commenting on an earlier tradition.

**D. Organ-by-organ reconciliation.** For heart, liver, spleen, kidney, brain, senses, lungs, larynx, long bones and glands, prepare chronological claim clusters with source IDs. Compare explicitly **across timescales** and **across genres**: a planetary analogy is not an organ's date of origin; a karmic relation is not a current physiological function; a group of hierarchies working in an organ system is not exclusive ownership of a named organ.

**E. Structured atlas integration.** Link each organ dossier to existing stable taxonomy IDs, with typed graph edges, evidence status and external source URL. Do not create a parallel duplicate of the Archive or renumber existing taxonomy topics. ## 16.1 What we learn by reading GA 314 and GA 316 together

Steiner gives at least three different answers to the question ‘what is an organ?’ In GA 314 an organ system is a radiating centre expressing the physical, etheric, astral or I-organization. In GA 316 (3 January) liver and heart are ‘sense organs’ in sharply different senses. In GA 316 (7 January) inner organs are forms whose production is attributed to higher beings and to formative processes of air and sound. These are not interchangeable answers. The concordance therefore separates **functional agency**, **spiritual perception analogy**, and **cosmic genesis or formal causation**.

### GA 314, 27 October 1922, morning — a fourfold map of bodily activity

Steiner begins with the order in which he would teach physiology: digestion first, then heart/lungs, kidneys in relationship to the nerve–sense apparatus, and finally liver/gallbladder/spleen. The lecture then gives an active fourfold sequence. Digestion, treated under the physical organization, alters the nature of incoming substances. The heart–lung system is presented as mediating their passage into etheric life through oxygen. The kidney system, in its ‘airy’ aspect rather than merely its anatomical excretion, is said to radiate the astral organization through the human being. The liver–gallbladder system is then said to mediate the ‘I’ through warmth and hydrogen. These correspondences are explicitly stated, but **not** exclusive descriptions of the organs’ biochemical functions.

The kidney passage especially complicates an organ-ownership model: Steiner says urinary excretion is secondary to his proposed higher kidney function. His discussion of urea, uric acid, temperature and nitrogen is a claim about possible investigation in 1922, not established proof of astral activity. The liver–gall system is not identified with the modern psychological ego; it is described as an organ-system through which an esoteric ‘I-organization’ becomes active in the organism. The first source should be read in §§ describing kidney radiations and subsequent liver–gall/warmth differentiation. [Direct lecture](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/FundAnthro/19221027a01.html).

### GA 314, 27 October 1922, afternoon — organ influences reverse direction in childhood

The continuation warns against treating those four assignments as straight one-way streams. Steiner introduces three *physical systems*: nerve–sense, rhythmic (including heart/lung) and metabolic–limb. He describes a four-to-one relationship between pulse and breathing, using the rhythmic system as a place where metabolic and nerve–sense activity meet. Organ shaping is portrayed as partly radial: liver and kidney activity strives outward, while plastic forces from the head round and restrain it, illustrated by the lungs. During the first seven years the kidney-associated astral and liver–gall-associated I activities are said to **become effective through reflection from the head**, not merely by streaming upward unchanged. The individual ear is explicitly said to involve the interaction of all four higher members as well as breathing, blood and metabolic rhythms.

An important **safety-critical** passage interprets tumours as misplaced attempts to form sense organs and goes on to propose mistletoe injections and fever responses. This is catalogued as Steiner’s historical claim only. It is **not** a modern explanation of cancer and **must not** be used to plan cancer care. [Direct lecture, notably §§4–10 and §§24–25](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA314/English/MP1986/19221027p01.html).

### GA 316, 3 January 1924 — liver and heart as contrasted ‘sense organs’

Here Steiner calls the **liver** an inward sense for discriminating the qualitative value of incoming food, while the **heart** is a spiritual sense for the organism’s inner life, particularly what the astral and I are doing. In the same discussion he rejects the conventional picture of the heart as a pump. This physiological denial is inconsistent with established cardiology. The Atlas records the historical statement without endorsing it. His larger analogy is about what an organ is *for*, not merely its microscopic appearance: he distinguishes looking at an object from understanding its purpose within a whole.

Earlier in the lecture he proposes a polarity: nutrition and etheric proliferation build up; astral influences restrain growth and permit consciousness; the I allegedly introduces an inner destructive process. These are claims about how higher members work within organs, not a birth-date or planet correspondence. [Direct lecture, §§7–9 and §§13–16](https://rsarchive.org/Medicine/YoungDoctors/19240103p01.html).

### GA 316, 4 January 1924 — organ form and matter in process

Steiner explicitly says **every organ** balances earthly substance forces against cosmic-peripheral forces. He contrasts the skull (predominantly cosmic) with the long bones of arms and legs (predominantly earthly), placing ribs and vertebrae between them. His femur example is more subtle than ‘bone has carbonate and phosphate’: he says the **current action** of a constituent (building versus being broken down) matters more than its simple presence. He uses the example of two people sitting on a bench at two separate times, where identical observed positions conceal different histories. This is a methodological distinction in the lecture. His specific chemical assignments and cosmic causal mechanisms are not established skeletal science. [Direct lecture, §§3 and 7–10](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/YoungDoctors/19240104a01.html).

### GA 316, 7 January 1924 — builders, organs, and the modes of knowing

This is a crucial bridge from evolutionary history to currently active organ intelligence. He says bones reflect *cosmic thought*, muscles are associated with a living fluidity accessible to imagination, and inner organs with air and sound-like formative patterns accessible to inspiration. He explicitly differentiates the agencies: **elemental spirits** in the skeletal system; **higher beings** in muscles; **still higher beings** involved with inner organs. These statements do **not** supply the specific names of an angel or archangel for the liver, heart or kidney. The absence of those assignments must remain visible.

He also relates **intuition** to the activity of organs within a differentiated warmth organization, distinguishing an organ’s shape from its ongoing activity. In the same lecture, the ear is said to make conscious vibrations involving the entire organism. The overall progression is not ‘each organ belongs to one celestial person’ but rather: physical structure → supersensible formative forces → present organ activity, with different kinds of investigation for each. [Direct lecture, §§4–16](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/YoungDoctors/19240107p01.html).

## 18.2 A more exact fourfold physiology: GA 314 morning

The morning lecture supplies a remarkably explicit set of correspondences. Steiner discusses solid/physical, fluid/etheric, gaseous/astral and warmth/I organizations, then relates the same sequence to the digestive tract and carbon, heart–lung processes and oxygen, kidney activity and nitrogen, and liver–gall activity and hydrogen. These are relationships within his proposed physiological model, not four verified scientific mechanisms. Nor is he identifying a different spiritual being as sole builder of each organ.

He begins with digestion, the transformation of nourishment and its passage through chyle and lymph into the blood. He then claims the heart–lung system makes a transition to living/etheric organization possible through oxygen. He invokes angelic beings in an illustration of what the organism would be like without physical oxygenation; this should not be recoded as an assertion that Angels uniquely formed the heart or lungs.

His kidney account is more unusual. He describes the kidney’s excretion as secondary to a supposed underlying astral radiation that gives sentient qualities to the organism. He proposes observations of urea and uric acid and differences in environmental temperature as a way of testing this proposal. The archive must preserve this assertion as historical spiritual physiology, not as established renal function.

He then connects the liver–gall system to differentiation of warmth and the I organization. He proposes a carbon–oxygen–nitrogen–hydrogen parallel. In the German original the expressions Nierensystem and Leber-Gallensystem concern a system or complex of functions. The index must not silently narrow the latter to the liver alone.

*Source: https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/FundAnthro/19221027a01.html | German comparison: https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA314/Compare_06.php?version=MP*

## 18.3 Why the head changes the meaning of kidney and liver activity

In the afternoon lecture, especially §§4, 7–10, Steiner turns the original organ associations into a theory of reciprocal activity. The kidney/astral system and liver–gall/I system are said to send radiating formative influences outward. The head and nerve-sense organization send counteracting, rounding, or sculptural influences in return. He explains the shape of the lungs as an encounter between a radial liver–kidney activity and a head-derived tendency to form rounded surfaces.

He then claims that before the change of teeth the liver and kidney radiations are not effective simply by proceeding upward. Instead, they are reflected from the head and act back downward. This is why the seemingly conflicting phrases “I organization proceeds from the liver” and “I-related processes proceed from the head” are, in his own explanation, intended to describe different moments in one interaction. This does not prove the mechanism, but it is important for fair interpretation of his text.

His account also has a developmental dimension: the first seven years are assigned principally to head/nerve-sense predominance, ages roughly seven to puberty to the rhythmic heart–lung system, and post-puberty to the metabolic-limb system. His proposed four-to-one relation between pulse and breath is extended analogically to the differential tempos of the bodily systems. These periodizations are not evidence-based clinical classifications.

Crucially, §24 uses the ear as a demonstration that all four organizational members and the three functional systems operate in each single organ. Therefore our concordance should describe organ-to-member associations as privileged functions, not mutually exclusive memberships.

*Source: https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA314/English/MP1986/19221027p01.html §§4–10, 13–16 and 24. German comparison: https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA314/Compare_07.php?version=MP*

## 18.4 Head, skull, long bones and the process of substance: GA 316 January 4

Steiner proposes that each bodily organ is formed through a balance between earth-related material forces and forces streaming from the cosmic periphery (§3). In his account the skull manifests cosmic predominance, limb long bones earthly predominance, and ribs and vertebrae an intermediate relation (§7). He invokes the partial buoyant support of the brain in cerebrospinal fluid (§4) as an illustration of this larger framework, then makes additional claims about the brain remaining internally at rest during human movement; those latter claims should not be adopted as physical fact.

In §§9–10 he analyzes the femur, its shaft and rounded head, invoking calcium carbonate and calcium phosphate as differentiated formative processes. He argues that detecting a substance in a location is not enough to understand what it does there. His example of two men seen at the same bench in the morning and afternoon makes the point: one may have remained seated while the other may have traveled in the interim. A snapshot of presence does not disclose a history of activity.

The English text prints CaPO3 as calcium-phosphate notation. This is not to be silently recast as a valid modern chemical formula or cited as a chemically accurate representation of bone mineral. Record the historical notation verbatim in a narrowly scoped source field and flag the need for source-text and chemistry review.

The same lecture extends its formative-process theory to fluoride and teeth, iron in blood, lead-elimination, magnesium processes around dentition and puberty, muscular consolidation, and alleged cosmic ages of graphite, diamond and coal. Some assertions about substances and human disease are demonstrably inaccurate or unsupported. The evidence index classifies them as historical substance-process or therapeutic statements, not verified biology.

# Phase 8 — The differentiated organism: organs, etheric currents, and consciousness

**Research date:** 2 November 2026. **Scope:** selected passages in nine additional lectures: GA 313 I–III (11–13 April 1921), GA 315 II–IV (13–15 April 1921), GA 317 II–IV (26–28 June 1924). This is a *selective first reading*, not a claim of complete audit of these nine lectures. The source-linked data file currently has **49 claim records** with precise numbered-English paragraph cues. None of these newly sampled lectures has undergone complete English section-by-section extraction or original-German collation.

## 25. Why the new lectures matter to the organ atlas

The earlier research established several very different questions: (a) when the seed of an organ was first produced in planetary evolution; (b) which spiritual beings Steiner associates with its production; (c) which planetary or elemental analogies he gives; (d) what a present organ allegedly does; and (e) what he claims it contributes during sleep or between incarnations. The GA 313–317 material supplies a sixth perspective: how bodily subsystems operate as **mutually affecting regions of one organism**. It also shows why the same organ may have many apparently divergent descriptions.

In GA 313 I, Steiner distinguishes a *primary* process from an *imprint* of a higher member. He is not assigning one spiritual body to one organ forever. In GA 313 II, the head and metabolic-limb regions supposedly receive different ethers from above and below. GA 317 II then moves from the head as the synthesis of organ activity to the brain as a reflector, rather than a source, of what he calls living cosmic thoughts. These are linked by Steiner's worldview but are **not identical mechanisms**.

A simple research rule follows: do not turn a lecture's statement about an organ's activity into a statement about its Old Saturn origin or its exclusive builder. Index the relation type, the timescale, the relevant system, and the exact source.

## 26. GA 313 (11–13 April 1921): the four members are not distributed by ownership

### 26.1 Head, chest, and metabolic-limb system (GA 313 I)

Steiner explains that head, chest and limbs are different concentrations of activity that nevertheless interpenetrate. In GA 313 I §§4–9 he distinguishes what is *primarily active* and what appears as an *imprint*. For the head, the physical organization is the primary process; he describes the etheric, astral and I organizations as having left imprints. For respiratory rhythm, he describes the physical and etheric as actively interwoven while I and astral aspects are imprints. For the metabolic-limb system, physical, etheric and astral organizations are primarily interrelated while the I leaves an imprint. None of those formulae means that the other members are absent.

A second claim emerges in §17: from the head, the differentiating influences of the organism are said to be distributed into particular organs, explicitly including heart and liver. Steiner contrasts this with forces that gather and unify the organism from below. This helps explain why the earlier GA 314 child-development lecture can call the liver an I-related organ while also describing hepatic activity as reflected by the head.

Steiner's method in §§2–3 is to regard matter as a process 'come to rest.' He applies this to silica/calcium analogies in the head and to differences between the presence of a substance and its physiological activity (§§6, 10–11, 19). The claim that magnesium is useless in milk or that silica works from hair toward the organism is not established physiology. Its historical significance is the distinction Steiner is trying to make between static chemical inventory and purported formative activity.

**Primary:** [GA 313 I, April 11, 1921, §§2–11 and 17–19](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA313/English/SOL/19210411.html).

### 26.2 Etheric currents and the eye (GA 313 II)

In GA 313 II §2 Steiner says the I manifests through temperature differentiation in the head and through posture, movement and equilibrium in the limbs. In §§3–5, he explicitly rejects an amorphous etheric body, distinguishing warmth, light, chemical and life ethers. In §7 he calls the eye a form organized by light. He then claims the head is especially permeable to warmth and light ethers (§8), whereas chemical and life ethers enter through the metabolic-limb region and pass upward (§9).

These four ethers thus enter the atlas as **directional, present-life formative relations**, distinct from Old Sun as a cosmic epoch or a claim that the biological eye originates exclusively from the physical Sun. He also offers a theory of malnutrition in §10 in which stomach and liver retain ethers that supposedly ought to circulate. That is a historical esoteric explanation, not a clinically supported cause of malnutrition.

**Primary:** [GA 313 II, April 12, 1921, §§2–10](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA313/English/SOL/19210412.html).

### 26.3 Chest, lungs and heart as balance (GA 313 III)

In GA 313 III §§2–3, the chest is not a closed subsystem. Its physical–etheric rhythmic process is allegedly influenced from the head and the metabolism, while astral and I organizations work into it. Thus his account of breathing and circulation describes *balance between regions*, not simply a heart containing one planet and lungs another. In §§7–8 he adds historical psychosomatic assertions linking prolonged worry and metabolic imbalance with disruption of bodily rhythms; no diagnosis or therapy should be inferred from these speculative etiologies.

**Primary:** [GA 313 III, April 13, 1921, §§2–3, 7–8](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA313/English/SOL/19210413.html).

## 27. GA 315 (13–15 April 1921): speech, movement, and bodily function

The first GA 315 lecture in Version 7 described the larynx as a metamorphosis of the posterior head and rib cage. The next three lectures extend that picture without providing an additional precise planetary genesis chronology. GA 315 II (§1) describes vowel speech as inwardly retained movement; the famous I and U gestures are linked respectively to walking and standing (GA 315 II §§4, 7). The symbolic five-vowel formula in §18 includes an E movement allegedly impressing the I into the etheric body.

GA 315 III §§1–6 contrasts inward vowels with outward consonants and pictures eurythmy as external movement polar to the larynx's reflected act of speech. These passages belong under **speech/movement metamorphosis** and **soul expression**, not organ builder or cosmic origin. His phylogenetic speculations about the priority of the vowel A (§15) are not established by linguistics or evolutionary biology.

GA 315 IV presents a detailed list of proposed treatments by movement and speech sound: B/P for kidney and post-intestinal digestive activity (§2), D/T for bowel functions (§3), G/K/Q for intestinal peristalsis (§4), F/V for urination (§6), and N for bowel rhythms (§13). These are specific claims that Steiner made, useful for a textual concordance but **not validated medical prescriptions**. The Atlas must expose a clearly marked filter for historical therapeutic assertions so they are never confused with medically supported treatment.

**Primary sources:** [GA 315 II, April 13](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA315/English/SOL/19210413.html); [GA 315 III, April 14](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA315/English/SOL/19210414.html); [GA 315 IV, April 15](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA315/English/SOL/19210415.html).

## 28. GA 317 (26–28 June 1924): thinking, the eye, and boundaries of organs

### 28.1 World thought and the brain (GA 317 II)

In GA 317 II §§11–15, Steiner says the nervous-sensory organization does not *produce* thought from itself. Rather, in his proposed spiritual cosmology, living thoughts exist in a universal ether and the developing brain makes possible their reflection into everyday consciousness. This resonates with his earlier discussions of 'world mind' and 'world thought,' but the concordance must keep a **specific brain/ether reflection claim** separate from wider Kabbalistic analogies.

In §§20–25, he links the child's proposed etheric development to the liver's role in willing and describes an alleged child who forms intentions but cannot bring them into action. This is his historical theory, not a reliable diagnosis of a child, and the surrounding karmic claims must not be used to judge children or families.

**Primary:** [GA 317 II, June 26, 1924, §§11–15 and 20–25](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA317/English/SOL/19240626.html).

### 28.2 Earth, light, warmth and the eye (GA 317 III)

In §§6–11 Steiner connects the I to gravity and earthly physical forces, while the astral organization is linked to warmth, light, chemism and life ether. The eye serves as an example of an outward-facing organ through which the organism and surrounding world meet. This is another body/world relationship, not a claim about which named angel fashioned the retina. In §§12–13 he offers a purported explanation of epilepsy in terms of the I and astral activity becoming trapped against organ boundaries, including lungs and brain. Such a theory is medically incorrect and must remain purely a historical record.

**Primary:** [GA 317 III, June 27, 1924, §§6–13](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA317/English/SOL/19240627.html).

### 28.3 Organ surfaces and the two supposed imbalances (GA 317 IV)

In GA 317 IV §§5–7, Steiner proposes two opposed conditions: an organ may hold too much astral and I organization inwardly, allegedly leading to spasm or pain, or may become too permeable so those members extend beyond its surface, allegedly accompanied by physical secretion. A later passage (§16) compares expansion beyond an organ to the spiritual process he believes occurs in dying. These are **organ-boundary claims**. They are not the same as the GA 205 organ-surface memory reflection or the GA 316 account of air/sound shaping organ forms.

**Primary:** [GA 317 IV, June 28, 1924, §§5–7 and 16](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA317/English/SOL/19240628.html).

# PHASE 9 | Auditing the moving human organism: GA 313 and GA 317

## 31. Research question and source-control status

This phase deepens the *source-level* comparison of Rudolf Steiner’s teachings on organ differentiation and the changing relationship of the human being’s bodily and supersensible organizations. Six lectures were revisited: GA 313, April 11–13, 1921, and GA 317, June 26–28, 1924. Their English renderings contain **164 numbered sections in all**: 21 + 30 + 20 for GA 313, and 33 + 26 + 34 for GA 317. A numbered-section census was established, but the census itself must **not** be confused with a complete reading or a complete organ-claim extraction. Across the previous selected-passages pass and this pass, 89 sections have at least one selected-passage examination; 75 have only their numbered location registered. None of these six lectures has yet earned a certified exhaustive bilingual/original-German audit.

Two research controls govern this edition. First, **an organ's cosmic origin**, its **ongoing formative processes**, its **relationship to particular spiritual members**, and its **function in sleep or consciousness** are distinct questions. Second, the descriptions remain **Steiner’s historical esoteric assertions**, not established physiological, genetic, psychological or clinical mechanisms. In particular, the medical claims and disability characterizations in GA 317 are scientifically unsupported and must not be applied to real individuals.

Primary sources: [GA 313 Lecture I, 11 April 1921](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA313/English/SOL/19210411.html); [Lecture II, 12 April](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA313/English/SOL/19210412.html); [Lecture III, 13 April](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA313/English/SOL/19210413.html); [GA 317 Lecture II, 26 June 1924](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA317/English/SOL/19240626.html); [Lecture III, 27 June](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA317/English/SOL/19240627.html); [Lecture IV, 28 June](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA317/English/SOL/19240628.html).

## 32. GA 313: the same four members act differently in different bodily regions

The first lecture makes a crucial classification that helps prevent errors in the organ concordance. The **head**, as a *nerve–sense organization*, is said to exhibit primary activity of the physical body, while etheric, astral and I organizations appear through their 'imprints' (§§4–5). The **chest** or rhythmic organism is said to display interplay of physical and etheric members as its primary process, with astral and I activity appearing as imprints (§§8–9). The **limb–metabolic organization** is said to show a primary interplay of physical, etheric and astral members, with the I leaving its imprint (§§8–9). These are not statements that the other members are absent in a region. They are distinctions between **primary activity** and **imprint**, which are terms of Steiner’s own explanatory model.

Steiner insists that an 'imprint' is not a plaster cast or a fixed anatomical shape. In the second lecture (§2), he says the I’s imprint may be **dynamic**: in the limbs it appears in balance and upright movement; in the head he associates it with the regionally differentiated warmth conditions. Therefore the research index should distinguish a **physical anatomical part**, a **functional system**, an **organizing process**, and a **spiritual member**. This also explains why a chest system can relate to both etheric and astral organization without implying contradictory exclusive ownership.

The first lecture (§§2–3) introduces a method central to Steiner’s organ descriptions: physical matter is approached as a process temporarily 'come to rest'. Applied to the silica and limestone discussion (§6), he juxtaposes an alleged *dynamic force-process* and a *material deposit* as opposite tendencies. Later (§§10–11), he claims silica has greater **material expression** in the head but greater **force activity** in the lower organism; these are separate relation types, not a literal discovery about anatomical silica functions. In §17 he explicitly says head-directed differentiating formative influences become apportioned to individual organs including **heart and liver**. This is a **present organizational account**—not an organ's Old Saturn or Old Sun date of origin.

Source: [GA 313 I, §§4–11, 16–19](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA313/English/SOL/19210411.html).

## 33. Four ether streams: not four physical substances

GA 313 II (§§3–9) explicitly differentiates *warmth ether*, *light ether*, *chemical ether* and *life ether*. In Steiner’s account the eye is organized through light, while the watery or fluid organization of the head is an imprint permeable to etheric influence (§7). He then describes warmth/light ether as particularly related to the head and chemical/life ether as acting upward from the metabolic-limb regions (§§8–10). These are not universally accepted states of matter and should not be conflated with the modern physics of heat, light or chemistry.

His important caution is that one should not imagine the etheric organization as a single undifferentiated substance. In §3 he compares that mistake with imagining stomach, heart and liver as one shapeless mass. An atlas that maps an 'etheric body' to one organ without qualifications would repeat the very oversimplification that he criticizes.

The later illness explanations in this lecture, such as a theory that malnutrition results from stomach or liver 'holding back' certain ethers, are historical conjectures rather than clinical explanations. The archive should classify them under **historical pathological theory**, not clinical advice.

Source: [GA 313 II, §§3–11](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA313/English/SOL/19210412.html).

## 34. A substantive correction: sleeping is not an undifferentiated withdrawal

In GA 313 II, §26, Steiner explicitly qualifies the familiar simplified picture in which the astral body and I entirely 'leave the body' during sleep. He describes them **withdrawing from the head and respiratory system** while becoming **more deeply engaged with the metabolic and circulatory organization**. The text presents this as a change of distribution or a rearrangement across the organism, which he compares with the alternation of day and night. This claim should be stored as **sleep_region_specific_reorganization**.

That is much more precise than saying that all spiritual activity ceases in all organs while asleep. It also alters the interpretation of organ intelligence: in Steiner’s worldview, *ongoing processes* can be distributed between bodily systems even when ordinary waking self-consciousness is interrupted. This source does not, by itself, give a separate named Angel or Archangel as builder of each affected organ. It should not be forced into the spiritual hierarchy mapping.

A nearby four-line schematic in §25 associates **I—death**, **astral body—illness**, **etheric body—health**, and **physical body—nutrition**. This is Steiner's speculative account of human constitution, **not a verified medical law**; the indexing model must record the scheme accurately without suggesting that human illness is medically caused by an astral body.

Source: [GA 313 II, §§19–26](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA313/English/SOL/19210412.html).

## 35. GA 313 III: the chest is a meeting place, not an isolated box

In the third lecture (§§1–3), Steiner describes chest organs as an unusually intimate physical–etheric unity, while the astral and I can, in his terminology, permeate that unity. He treats the heart-and-breathing rhythms as a **meeting point of head, metabolism and surrounding environment** (§§7–10). This differs from a model where each organ is assigned an exclusive spiritual 'owner'. A significant but medically unsubstantiated set of passages associates persistent worries, hasty thoughts, hunger and thirst with later rhythmic disease (§§7–8).

Crucially, in §14 his discussion of germs minimizes their causal role in infections. The concordance should preserve his actual claim as a **historical error** rather than representing it as an accepted description: infectious microbes do cause infectious diseases. The related suggestions about high-altitude environments and therapies (§15 onward) also belong to historical treatment assertions, not recommendations.

Source: [GA 313 III, §§1–15](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA313/English/SOL/19210413.html).

## 36. GA 317: the eye and brain in the relation of world and consciousness

GA 317 II (§§12–16) adds a very different layer: Steiner says thoughts exist in the 'world ether' before the individual's present thinking. The **brain** and nervous system are described as reflective instruments through which living thoughts take the form of ordinary mental images. He explicitly makes an analogy with a milk jug that receives milk but does not produce it. **The reflective function** is an assertion about cognition; it must not be misread as evidence of a brain's cosmic creation date. His theory of nervous tissue continuously being 'killed off' in thought is not modern neuroscience.

In GA 317 III (§§7–11), Steiner says the waking **I** interacts with gravity and other terrestrial forces, while the **astral organization** is associated with some warmth, light, chemical and life-ether activity. The **eye** is treated as an especially direct interface with external physical processes (§11). This is a **sense-interface model**, not a claim that eyes were anatomically made by named Thrones or Archangels in this lecture.

Sources: [GA 317 II, §§12–16](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA317/English/SOL/19240626.html); [GA 317 III, §§7–11](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA317/English/SOL/19240627.html).

## 37. Organ boundaries: keep metaphor, causal claims and clinical error separate

GA 317 III–IV (§§12–13, and IV §§3–7) offers two supposed abnormalities of astral/I activity near organ surfaces: **retention or congestion** within an organ and **excessive permeability or outward passage** through an organ. Steiner uses the first to explain epileptic convulsions and the second to explain symptoms including secretion. These are *historical esoteric explanatory pictures*, not valid explanations of epilepsy, mental illness, bedwetting, pain or bowel problems. His claim in GA 317 IV §14 that bedwetting reveals outward 'astral leakage' is specifically **not a medical diagnosis**.

Particularly concerning, GA 317 III §22 includes moral-character claims based on external skull or organ features and advocacy of phrenological readings. Phrenology and such moral/physiological determinations are invalid and can stigmatize vulnerable children. Present this material as historical evidence of Steiner's claims—not as an actionable psychological model. The compiler has added explicit safety flags to the new passage records.

Sources: [GA 317 III, §§12–14, 22](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA317/English/SOL/19240627.html); [GA 317 IV, §§3–7, 14](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA317/English/SOL/19240628.html).

## 10.1 Why this pass changes the organ concordance

Steiner opens the 11 April lecture with a methodological distinction. Material substances, he claims, should be considered the **resting products of processes**. His German terminology includes `Vorgang`, *Prozeß*, and the expression *zur Ruhe gekommen*. He applies this distinction to silica, calcium, magnesium and fluorine. It is not enough, in his view, to know that a material is present in a particular organ: one must ask whether it is supposedly forming, sustaining, differentiating, or being eliminated. The underlying physiological hypotheses are not scientifically verified, but his own *explanatory method* is now clear.

In §§4–9 of the same lecture he distinguishes **three functional regions** (head / rhythmic middle / metabolic-limb) and **four constitutional members** (physical, etheric, astral, I). He does not arrange them in a one-to-one list. He says all the members participate throughout the human being, with differing degrees and modes of activity. At the head, the physical organization is described as directly active and the other members as *imprints*; in the rhythmic chest, physical and etheric are described as more directly interwoven; and in the metabolic-limb realm he describes physical, etheric and astral interplay, with a different imprint of I.

| Region or mode | Direct/primary activity in Steiner's formulation | Presented as imprints (not absent) | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Head / nerve-sense | Physical organization | Etheric, astral, I | GA 313, Apr 11, §§4–5 and 9 |
| Rhythmic respiratory system | Physical + etheric cooperation | Astral and I | GA 313, Apr 11, §§8–9 |
| Metabolic-limb system | Physical + etheric + astral interplay | I in a distinctive dynamic sense | GA 313, Apr 11, §§8–9; Apr 12, §2 |

The German source uses *`Abdruck`* for *imprint*; he also calls the physical process **primär wirksam**, or directly/primarily active, in the head. **An imprint is not a mere absence**. Steiner says the I can leave an imprint in the body's *systems of forces*: for example, the differentiated thermal conditions of the head, or the forces of balance and walking in the limbs (Apr 12, §2). That is why assigning a human member to an organ must specify the **relation type**: primary activity, imprint, reflection, formative counterforce, and so forth.

## 10.2 A worked example: why the liver and heart are different but interdependent

On April 11 (§17), Steiner says that formative forces active from the head have a *differentiating* effect, distributing activity into distinct organs, explicitly including **heart** and **liver**. His image contrasts this differentiating head-to-body influence with a lower-body force tending to unify bodily fluids (§§15–18). This is a third account of the organs, distinct from their Old Saturn cosmic rudiments and planetary symbols. It is a present-life model of the human body's **organization of differences**.

The liver's special I/warmth relation in GA 314 (Oct 27, 1922) and its inward nutritional-sense characterization in GA 316 (Jan 3, 1924) should therefore not be treated as contradictory. They answer different questions: the liver's relation to a spiritual member, its alleged perceptive function, and its participation in a distribution of formative activity from head to organs. Similarly, the heart's Old Saturn/Leo association in GA 110 is not the same *kind* of claim as its later rhythmic or inner-sense role.

**Important interpretive boundary:** In GA 313 I §§12–13 Steiner introduces plant combustion and ashes as a claimed polarity to the respiratory and circulatory processes, then uses this for proposed therapeutic responses. Those are specific historical assertions and not independently demonstrated treatments.

## 10.3 GA 313 II: the etheric body cannot be drawn as a shapeless cloud

In the second lecture (§3) Steiner says it would make no sense to describe the stomach, heart, and liver as one indistinct anatomical mass; likewise, he argues, the etheric organization should not be pictured as an undifferentiated mist. He then distinguishes four hypothetical ethers in §§4–5:

| Term in Steiner | His conceptual role | Key caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warmth ether / *`Wärmeäther`* | Upper or exterior formative influence, also related to climatic conditions | Not a measured independent physical field |
| Light ether / *`Lichtäther`* | Associated with the head and the eye; the eye alleged to have arisen out of light | Not a validated explanation of eye development |
| Chemical ether / *`chemischer Äther`* | Inward-acting, conceived as polar to ordinary chemistry | Not ordinary chemical synthesis or analysis |
| Life ether / *`Lebensäther`* | Organic vitality and the lower/inner ether stream | Not a biologically identified substance |

In §§7–12 Steiner imagines an interaction of **warmth/light ethers arriving predominantly through the head** with **chemical/life ethers ascending through the metabolic-limb system**. That gives his organs a further dimension: they are, within his system, places where distinct streams and organizations meet. We should index these as *regional ether relations*, not as evidence that a unique hierarchy created the eye or stomach on a specific cosmic planet.

Steiner attempts to apply this model to malnutrition and other illnesses (§§10–12). These asserted causal explanations are unsupported. The concordance marks them **historical medical hypotheses**, not accepted clinical descriptions.

## 10.4 The sleep correction: not a uniform departure of I and astral body

The second lecture's §26 gives one of the clearest corrections to simplified retellings of Steiner's sleep teaching. His German calls it **Umlagerung**, meaning *rearrangement or redistribution*. The claim is that in sleep the I and astral activity become **less closely attached to the head and respiratory organization**, while becoming **more intensely involved in metabolism and circulation**. He compares this with the shifting distribution of day and night around Earth. Hence a blanket statement that these two members simply leave the whole body at night omits an important qualification in his own lecture.

| State | Head and respiratory systems (Steiner's claim) | Metabolic and circulatory systems (Steiner's claim) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Waking | Closer I / astral relation to physical and etheric organizations | Comparatively less penetration |
| Sleeping | Looser I / astral relation | Greater penetration into physical and etheric organization |

This is a description of Steiner's claimed supersensible physiology; the spiritual bodies and their redistribution are not experimentally demonstrated. The following §§27–30 make further treatment claims involving sulfur, phosphorus, arsenic, and antimony. **The Archive's historical descriptions must never be interpreted as dosage or treatment guidance**, especially given the toxicity of some of these substances.

## 10.5 Four principles, four processes — not four proven clinical causes

The lecture's §§19–25 presents the following schema:

| Human member | Steiner's paired term |
| --- | --- |
| I | Death |
| Astral body | Illness |
| Etheric body | Health |
| Physical body | Nutrition |

This is a **metaphysical explanatory polarity**, not a scientifically established theory of disease. The categories are neither an organ-by-organ map nor a claim that illness in an individual person proves some kind of astral defect. Recording the table in a separate `constitutional_polarity` relation prevents accidental reinterpretation as a diagnosis.

# PHASE 11 — RHYTHMIC ORGANS AND THE COSMIC ORIGIN OF THOUGHT

## 11.1 Two lectures, two different dimensions of the organism

The April 1921 lecture explains the rhythmic organization—chiefly breathing and circulation—as a meeting point for influences that are not confined to the chest itself. The June 1924 lecture asks a different question: if thoughts are not generated within the brain in Steiner’s system, how can a human being possess and reflect them? Its answer invokes the world ether and an alleged pre-earthly history of human consciousness. These are related through Steiner’s wider spiritual anthropology, but should not be flattened into a single organ-to-planet diagram.

## 11.2 GA 313 III: the chest as an intersection, not a self-contained cause

In §§2–3, Steiner distinguishes physical–etheric cooperation in the chest from the mode of organization he attributes to the head. He insists that the equilibrium of chest rhythms emerges through forces associated with the head on one side and metabolism on the other. The lecture therefore treats breathing and circulation as manifestations of several interacting streams rather than merely as autonomous chest processes. The word “meeting point” here is our editorial synthesis; it is not an additional spiritual entity.

At §5, he says part of the astral organization is outside the etheric during sleep yet astral activity still works through the lower organism. This is an important complement to GA 313 II §26 (Umlagerung): the latter describes a redistribution of I/astral activity, loosening its head/respiratory relation while increasing its participation in metabolism and circulation. The two passages must be indexed as closely related statements, rather than as two different hypotheses about the initial creation of the lungs or heart.

Sections 6–9 then use falling asleep and awakening as an alleged condensation of symptom complexes, while associating older worries, hasty thinking and persistent hunger or thirst with delayed effects on the rhythmic organism. These passages record Steiner’s medical theory, not a reliable method of diagnosing a particular person. A further distinction is necessary between a non-clinical historical claim that emotional life may affect health in general, and Steiner’s specific unvalidated organ-causation model.

## 11.3 Where the lecture moves beyond anatomy: sunlight, magnetism and infection

GA 313 III §§10–20 draws an extended analogy between terrestrial and extra-terrestrial influences and the human rhythmic organization. Steiner discusses differences between tropical and polar conditions, sunlight, elevation, burning plant material, electricity and magnetism. These are not simply statements of spiritual hierarchy: they are attempts to derive therapeutic interventions from a speculative environmental cosmology. His proposed treatments of arrhythmia and tuberculosis using magnetic fields, and his rejection of germs as actual pathogens, are scientifically unsupported or false. The concordance labels them separately so source fidelity does not become accidental medical advice.

## 11.4 GA 317 II: how living thought allegedly becomes ordinary thinking

The key passage sequence is §§11–16. Steiner first distinguishes the synthetic character he attributes to brain organization from the analytic activity he attributes to metabolism and limbs. He then uses the milk-jug analogy: a vessel does not manufacture what is poured into it. This is an explanatory analogy he uses to deny that the brain produces thoughts. It is not experimental evidence about cognition.

In §13, he identifies the universal world ether (Weltenäther) as the supposed bearer of living thoughts and says that the developing human etheric body receives them in pre-earthly existence. In §§14–15, he moves from cosmic thought to organ formation: he says the thought-life helps organize the brain and sensory nerves, which later operate as a mirror in which those thoughts appear as personal, everyday representations. The original German comparison supports the term Weltenäther and the Milchtopf (milk-jug) analogy. Importantly, this creates TWO distinct predicates: the brain is said to be FORMED BY living cosmic thought, and to REFLECT thought into ordinary subjective consciousness. Neither identifies a particular angel as its sole builder.

Steiner also calls nervous tissue a product of material breakdown and relates psychiatric symptoms to failures of reflection. These are not scientifically valid explanations of neurons, cognition or mental illness. In the concordance they remain historical views, and the mirror analogy is preserved as an analogy rather than being promoted into a biological mechanism.

## 11.5 The liver, intention and the danger of explanatory overreach

GA 317 II §9 repeats Steiner’s earlier hypothesis that a subtle abnormality of the liver can make it difficult to translate an intention into action. At §25 he further describes an allegedly defective etheric organization of the liver and connects the interpretation to the educator’s astral body. Sections 21–24 provide an educational “next-higher member” sequence; this is not an account of angels constructing the organ.

The later §§27–31 speculate that the astral and I organizations can have trouble entering the liver and stomach during incarnation, resulting in fluctuating experiences and behavioral difficulties. They also assign disability and psychological illness to hypothetical past-life conditions. Such accounts are unverified, and using them to explain a real child’s disability or character would be inappropriate and potentially harmful. For this reason, the dossier explicitly distinguishes source description from clinical legitimacy.

Steiner’s liver remarks now fall into at least seven documented relationship types: “inner Jupiter” symbolism (GA 128); I/warmth involvement (GA 314); food-value sense analogy (GA 316); head-differentiating formative activity (GA 313 I); allegedly stored future-life brain disposition (GA 205); will/intention (GA 317); and speculative childhood organ–soul interaction (GA 317 II). They cannot be represented responsibly by one symbol or a single unnamed “liver hierarchy”.

## Findings that change the Atlas structure

### One I, differentiated instruments

GA 313 IV §9 explicitly qualifies upper/lower I language as instruments of one I. This supports a graph with regional activities, rather than separate selves inhabiting different organs. Its neighboring account links nutrition, sensory experience and bodily warmth. These present-life relations remain distinct from the earliest cosmic formation of physical organs.

### Metamorphosis has more than one meaning

GA 313 VI separates a serial organ metamorphosis from a polar one. The distinction affects how the dossiers should be read:

| Relation being indexed | Principal organs | Source | Editorial boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Serial formative metamorphosis | Stomach, liver, lung, head/brain | VI §§4–6 | Not a literal anatomical conversion or verified evolutionary genealogy |
| Polar metamorphosis | Heart and lung | VI §14 | Not the same edge as serial transformation |
| Further transformed organ form | Uterus and heart | VI §14 | Not a claim that adult heart tissue becomes uterus |
| Etheric counterpart | Alleged male etheric uterus | VI §14 | Metaphysical assertion, not anatomical organ |
| Tissue-fluid formation/function | Heart | VI §§11–12 | Not Old Saturn chronology or named hierarchy agency |

The word **Urorgan** in the heart passage should not silently be converted into a dated cosmic origin. The surrounding discussion concerns formation through tissue fluid. Likewise, the liver's alleged inward spiritual activity (VI §§8–10) is a further relation type; it does not replace its earlier planetary, nutritive, will or future-life contexts.

### Bodily processes and spiritual activity

GA 313 V distinguishes arsenic-like force activity from the actual presence of arsenic (§2), before moving to distinct historical remedy proposals. A process analogy therefore needs its own predicate. Its phosphorus/blood account (§§15–17) adds a claimed I–blood-cell boundary, a striated-muscle association, and a tissue/bone degeneration hypothesis. No spiritual hierarchy is named as their exclusive builder.

The poisoning/death-retrospect comparison in V §18 and GA 317 IV's dying/outflow comparison belong to a comparative analogy layer. They are not interchangeable mechanisms or medical predictions. GA 313 VI's organ-imagination hypothesis (§21) likewise concerns purported involuntary pathology; it must not be presented as a safe method for deliberate supersensible development.

### Memory absorption is not organ-surface congestion

GA 317 V §12 expressly distinguishes excessive absorption of impressions from the organ-surface congestion of previous lectures. This prevents merging the two merely because both use inward/outward vocabulary. Its surrounding account places head and metabolic-limb organization in opposed constitutional arrangements, mediated by rhythm. These are schematic relations, not spatial shells established by anatomy. A diagram/deictic ambiguity around §5 remains unresolved; the explicit descriptions in §§5–7 are indexed without repairing the transcript by conjecture.

Hair-color/sulfur/iron classification, behavioral predictions about disability or mania, dietary treatments and verse-induced protein changes are recorded as unsupported historical claims. Their specificity must not be mistaken for diagnostic validity. GA 317 V §26 also contains an unnumbered third-person editorial account; the section audit includes it as context without attributing its exact words as Steiner's verbatim speech.

## Implications for organ development research

The new sources strengthen a distinction needed throughout this Atlas. Steiner's assertion that organs participate in inward spiritual activity is not identical with a claim that a student consciously perceives that activity. A pathological intrusion of purported organ imagery into consciousness, as described in GA 313 VI §21, is also not the same as trained Imagination, Inspiration or Intuition. The present passages do not establish a prescribed organ-visualization exercise or prove that awareness automatically confers the creative capacities of a spiritual hierarchy.

Likewise, a claim that the I transforms bodily processes must be indexed with its level of description: nutritional participation, childhood development, bodily warmth, deliberate moral/spiritual transformation or future metamorphosis. Conflating those levels would make a comprehensive organ history appear simpler than Steiner's actual, dispersed accounts. This edition adds present-function, constitutional-schema and metamorphosis evidence; it does not complete the cosmic chronology for each organ.

The strongest next improvements are full German collation, comparison with clearly sourced training passages, and continued reading of the remaining medical lectures. Any later cross-tradition comparison belongs in a separate editorial layer and cannot fill gaps in Steiner's own evidence.
