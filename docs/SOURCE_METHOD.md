# Editorial and source method

## Scope of evidence

- Use **Rudolf Steiner's own** books, articles, correspondence where relevant, recorded lectures, and directly attributable artistic instructions or works for the substantive teaching.
- Use the Rudolf Steiner Archive and official GA Complete Works catalogue to locate primary evidence and bibliographic metadata. Later commentary is **not** primary teaching evidence.
- Do **not** infer that a teaching appears in a particular lecture merely because the GA volume is listed as a reading lead.

## Evidence levels

1. `lead`: a GA volume, book or lecture series worth checking, not exact passage evidence.
2. `located`: dated lecture or chapter and working link found, but passage not fully checked.
3. `verified`: passage read, with edition, translation, chapter/lecture date and properly attributed quote or precise paraphrase.
4. `contested`: ambiguous attribution, variable translation or editorial reconstruction; explain uncertainty.

## Exact source record

Record GA number, title, whether authored or recorded lecture, dated lecture if applicable, chapter or section, significant original German terms, translation and edition, exact URL, relevant passage, evidence status, date checked and review notes.

## Essential distinctions

- Authored/revised books versus stenographic lectures and posthumous compilations.
- Steiner's terminology versus researcher-generated taxonomy labels.
- Spiritual-scientific claims versus independently established empirical findings.
- Historical cultural statements versus current evidence; identify discriminatory statements with context.
- Original quotation versus our paraphrase.

## Topic placement and revisions

Every idea has one **stable canonical topic ID** and one primary parent, with crosslinks for secondary contexts and aliases for synonyms. Do not silently renumber IDs. Every substantive change should have an exact source reference whenever possible and a documented review trail.
