## I. The fourfold physiology in GA 314, 27 October 1922 (morning)

Steiner's proposed sequence of medical study moves from digestion to heart/lungs, kidney–nerve/sense relationships, then liver/gall/spleen. He goes on to relate **digestive processes to the physical organization**, **heart–lung and oxygen processes to the etheric**, **kidney and nitrogen processes to the astral**, and **liver–gall, warmth and hydrogen to the I organization**. He calls renal excretion *secondary* to what he imagines as the kidney system's astral-radiating function. These are specific **organ-and-constitutional-member claims**, not an exclusive assignment of a spiritual being or a cosmological origin date to each organ.

[GA 314, 27 Oct 1922 morning](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/FundAnthro/19221027a01.html), especially the discussions of oxygen, kidney radiation and liver–gall differentiation.

## II. The threefold and fourfold systems interpenetrate in GA 314 (afternoon)

Steiner distinguishes nerve–sense, rhythmic and metabolic–limb systems. He describes the approximate four-to-one ratio of pulse and breath as indicative of a meeting of two different organizational tempos. He uses kidney and liver formative radiations meeting counteracting forces from the head to account for the shape of the lungs. **In children before the change of teeth**, he says kidney/astral and liver–gall/I influences act after reflection from the head. The *ear* illustrates that one localized organ includes all four organizational members and participation of metabolism, breathing and circulation. An exact concordance should record each of these **separate** claims.

The late sections propose tumours as misplaced sense-organ formations and contain mistletoe-related therapeutic assertions. These are historical esoteric claims, **not an evidence-based account of cancer and not treatment guidance**.

[GA 314, 27 Oct 1922 afternoon](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA314/English/MP1986/19221027p01.html), §§2–10 and §§24–25.

## III. Liver and heart as two different kinds of sense organ (GA 316, 3 January)

Steiner compares the liver to a sense organ for discriminating the *material value* of substances received as food, while he calls the heart a sense organ for the inward activity of the astral and I organizations. This **sense-organ analogy** differs from the GA 314 organ–member map. He also denies that the heart acts as a pump; modern cardiovascular science does establish the heart's pumping function, so this assertion must be accurately attributed as a historically incorrect medical claim rather than endorsed.

Earlier in this lecture he portrays the physical body/nutrition and etheric growth in polarity with the astral organization (restraint of growth, consciousness) and I organization (destruction). These statements concern **present ongoing function** rather than cosmic dates.

[GA 316, 3 Jan 1924](https://rsarchive.org/Medicine/YoungDoctors/19240103p01.html), §§7–9 and §§13–16.

## IV. From static constituents to formative processes (GA 316, 4 January)

Steiner proposes that every organ manifests a different balance between **earthly material forces** and **peripheral cosmic influences**. The skull represents the latter to a greater extent, long limb bones the former, ribs and vertebrae an intermediate balance. In discussing calcium carbonate and calcium phosphate he urges attention to a substance's activity—formation or breakdown—rather than its mere detectable presence. The two-men-on-a-bench analogy explicitly illustrates the gap between a snapshot and a history of process. This is a useful **methodological distinction** even though his specific cosmic and chemical explanations are not established physiology.

[GA 316, 4 Jan 1924](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/YoungDoctors/19240104a01.html), §§3, 7 and 9–10.

## V. Spiritual builders do not map one-to-one onto anatomical structures (GA 316, 7 January)

Steiner associates the **skeleton** with elementary spirits, the **muscles** with beings of a higher rank and **inner organs** with still higher spiritual beings. He aligns bone/definite contours with *thinking*, muscular and fluid metamorphosis with *imagination*, organs formed through air and sound patterns with *inspiration*, and the activity of organs in warmth with *intuition*. In this passage he does **not** identify a specific angel, archangel or named hierarchy as unique maker of the liver, lungs or kidneys.

The lung is described as having a **more direct** relation to formative breathing/air forces than other internal organs. He says hearing involves resonance of the entire organism, with the ear bringing it to consciousness. Separate records are needed for **spiritual agents**, **organ formative principles**, **sensory function** and **method of cognition**.

[GA 316, 7 Jan 1924](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/YoungDoctors/19240107p01.html), §§4–16.
