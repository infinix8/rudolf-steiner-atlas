## Attribution and corrections

GA314-10 §§27–28 introduces Palmer’s unnumbered clinical report. Patient outcome reports and ages belong to the named participants; Steiner’s questions do not convert them into his independently observed outcomes. German *Ende der zwanziger Jahre* answers an age question: the patient was in her late twenties, not living in the late 1920s during a 1924 discussion. [English and German](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA314/Compare_10.php).

GA314-16 interleaves Steiner’s numbered remarks with Wegman reading cases four and five from their coauthored medical book. The quoted case narrative is indexed separately without duplicating it as a new lecture. At §4 the mother and sister are the child’s mother and maternal aunt; the English possessive “my” is misleading. At §18 German refers to accessory organs that pass away in embryonic development, not to every embryonically developing organ. [Comparison](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA314/Compare_16.php).

## Ethical qualifications and invalid medical assertions

GA314-12 §52 explicitly rejects withholding treatment because disease might be karmic and opposes intrusion into individual karma. This qualifies the use of historical disease interpretations; it does not validate karmic causation. [German and English](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA314/Compare_12.php).

GA314-15 §§6–7 combines harmful unsupported assertions about vaccination with explicit practical acceptance of vaccination and rejection of fanatical refusal. Both statements remain visible. Similarly, remote-case qualifications in -11 §19 and -15 §41 must accompany theoretical diagnoses. [22 April](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA314/English/SOL/19240422.html).

The register flags erroneous infection causation, congenital syphilis distinctions, denial of syphilitic tabes causation, mercury detoxification, absolute mistletoe harmlessness, trauma-based breast cancer generalizations, and claims that surgery displaces cancer into pneumonia. Transgenerational allantois explanations and maternal-shock assertions are unsupported and can imply family blame. They are retained as historical textual evidence, never as medical explanations or protocols. No doses, injection preparations or treatment schedules are supplied in the research claims.
