## Development and bodily metamorphosis

GA315 VI §5 supplies an important developmental distinction: the forces allegedly recruited by bodily eurythmy appear as growth in childhood, while adult resistance to continued growth changes their expression into organ plasticity. Compare GA313 IX §6, indexed by ORG-CLAIM-V14-032, where imaginative cognition and plastic growth forces are related. The comparison concerns a proposed shared force, not identity of the exercises or a demonstrated biological mechanism. Vowels and circulatory rhythms also overlap the adjacent GA313 IX discussion, but VI's respiratory/digestive scope should remain explicit.

III §§20–24 combines a lunar account of head roundness with the localization of formative activity in speech and its alleged redistribution through will. Its etheric head-like figure is not a claim that a second physical head grows. No named spiritual hierarchy is assigned as a builder here. These passages extend the formative-development axis without supplying an Old Saturn date for any individual internal organ.

## Therapeutic correspondences and qualifications

The first-pass register contains 90 propositions: **66 provisional new candidates, 20 overlaps, three different-context comparisons and one apparent conflict** against inspected prior registers. These categories do not certify global novelty. The five vowel correspondences in II §18 are split into separate propositions rather than retained as one undifferentiated claim. The vowel I must also be distinguished from the I organization.

IV's sound/organ scheme is more differentiated than a generic movement-benefits-digestion claim: post-absorption/kidney activity, food processing, transport, gas, evacuation rhythm, transit and secretion are separately indexed. IV §17 adds an allegedly opposite etheric bowel current. This is an attributed esoteric account, not accepted peristaltic anatomy. German IV §6 itself says *Psychisches*, so the English psychological wording cannot simply be corrected to physiological without further evidence.

V §§1–4 differentiates judgment and feeling by relative I activity. Warmth in its circulatory account is explicitly not reducible to increased speed. Its B/migraine and M/abdominal-pain proposals are placed between episodes. V §§26–27 distinguishes reassessment after two or three days from the much longer period claimed for substantial effects. These are historical therapeutic assertions, not a clinical regimen.

VIII §§10–19 adds per-organ centrifugal/near-centripetal tendencies, cardiopulmonary/kidney interaction and compensatory heart irregularity, followed by explicit limits involving fractures and carcinoma. Those limits qualify its harmonization claims without validating them. Its rejection of organs devoted exclusively to soul functions belongs to that functional discussion; it is not sufficient to resolve everything Steiner says elsewhere about supersensible organs.

## Textual and attribution controls

Eleven selective German check entries cover all six comparison views. No whole lecture is certified bilingual, exhaustively atomized or independently second-reviewed. VI §13 contains an awkward ordinary-sleep comparison in German as well as English, with an editorial stenographic uncertainty. The new apparent-conflict link to GA317 III's I/astral interruption account deliberately remains unresolved. A special exercise state, the ordinary-sleep wording and the transmitted text are separate questions.

The thyroid/laterality question in V §§21–23 must not become a Steiner-endorsed thyroid mechanism. His reply denies a prescribed right emphasis for left-handed people. VIII §9 retains the A/S kidney-letter variant. IV's gaps and letter alternatives remain textual issues, not silently standardized prescriptions. VI §§25–29 are attributed to a heading and Friedrich Husemann; his Goethe/anatomy remarks and reported supervision warning are not direct Steiner propositions.

Racialized R/L explanations, national-language stereotypes, moralized angular or rounded organs, the motor-nerve denial, animalizing disability language and unvalidated disease mechanisms are flagged. They remain searchable historical evidence without being presented as medical or linguistic knowledge.
